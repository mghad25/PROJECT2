How the model works

Contributions. $100 a month from age 20 until 22. From 22: salary × savings rate. The rate rises by the Save More Tomorrow step each year up to the cap; salary rises by the real pay rise for 15 years, then 1% (2% for Lebanon).

Base case. Balancet+1 = Balancet × (1 + r) + contribution, with r the weighted expected real return minus fund costs. The FI number is yearly spending ÷ withdrawal rate (25× at 4%).

Portfolio risk. Volatility comes from the full covariance matrix: σ²ₚ = Σᵢ Σⱼ wᵢ wⱼ σᵢ σⱼ ρᵢⱼ, so diversification lowers it below the weighted average.

Simulation. Each year’s return is drawn from a normal distribution with mean r + σ²/2 and standard deviation σ. That centres median growth on the base case, so volatility drag is built in. A fixed random seed keeps results repeatable.

Confidence ages. The age by which the chosen share of the 2,000 paths has reached the FI number. The base case is the median, so it is reached on time in roughly half to 60% of futures.

What it leaves out. Fat tails and crash clustering, random inflation, taxes on withdrawals (none in the UAE; in Germany roughly 10% more capital needed, our estimate), currency moves, and broker or FX costs. The crash lab and stress tests cover what the normal distribution misses.
